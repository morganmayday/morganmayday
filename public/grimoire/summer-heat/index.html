summer heat

It’s laid flat on its back, the slight flush of heat under the light sheen of sweat, when I walk into the room. By the time I close the fridge, it’s propped itself up on the heels of its hands.

“Caroline,” she says, half-breathless with the heat of the room.

“That’s my name, don’t wear it out.” I cap the sports drink and toss it to her—she doesn’t bother catching it, just waits for it to stop rolling and snags it off the floor. “You holding up okay?” I know, of course; even if I couldn’t feel it, I can still see the line of heat shimmer where the stress meets her will. I still have to ask.

Adelaide grimaces and knocks back a mouthful. “I’m alive,” she answers, takes another gulp. “But fuck.”

“August is always the worst month,” I agree, and take the drink as she offers it back out. “Boiling alive and remembering.”

She groans, long and loud, running a hand through her hair—she’s not even wearing her glasses. The heat is really getting to her; distress and fatigue clear on her face. I enjoy the view, nevertheless. “Yeah. Fuck. It’s not even like it’s a helpful pressure.”

“Mm.” I finish the drink, let the bottle hang from my fingers. “You’re inventive. You could do something with it. Probably.”

“Is that worth it?” Adelaide asks. “Is that the kind of thing I want to use? The crucible, fueled by–by old pain? I like what I am now.” I wouldn’t want you or Leylune to go, she doesn’t say, and I fear that would happen.

“Well, this isn’t working.” I settle down next to her, lean my jaw against the table leg. “It still hurts, all these years later. Something’s gotta give. We’re stronger than this.” You know you don’t have to be scared of that, I don’t tell her, in turn.

“I don’t fucking like that I have to be strong.” Disgust, faint, in her grimace. “Can’t we just have PTSD? Like, you know, the medical condition we have?”

“Okay, fine,” I grumble. “I’ll do something.” I feel the tension in her shoulders lessen. God, I put so much weight on it, don’t I? I reach out, and pull it against my side, head slumping against my shoulder.

“…You should rest,” I suggest, for not the first time. “Take some time. Let Leylune take up the work you’ve been doing.”

She considers, for longer than she has before. “God. Maybe? Maybe. Not now,” she adds, betraying herself again, “but maybe after the heatwave.”

“Don’t you want to not have to deal with the heatwave?”

“She’ll—Luney won’t like it, either.”

I grip its shoulder for a moment, run my thumb over the edge of her shoulderblade. “Adelaide.”

“I know,” it mutters. “…I just…”

She doesn’t need to tell me. I don’t let her try, either.
