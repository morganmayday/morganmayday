taxonomies of rotting things

for a while, i’ve not had a particularly…detailed taxonomy of the metaphysical. i’ve stolen terms where necessary from those i read, but i’ve struggled with coining things myself, or even describing things in general, as i haven’t bothered using someone else’s taxonomy wholecloth.

this has been a glaring weakness in my ability to model.

the thing about names is that a good name is going to grip the thing it speaks of. to not have a name for something is to have less good a handle—a name shorthands description. if i can name a halo as such, and i can describe it on its own, i can symlink that word to that concept in my mind, attach an encapsulated denotative load. using someone else’s glossaries is…

a glossary is for two things: solidifying your denotative load, as putting things to text tends to do, and communicating that load without having to re-communicate it every time you reference the same concept. using others’ glossaries for my own definitions has built something piecemeal and haphazard. i engage with these things at a depth enough that something makeshift will no longer serve.

when i picked this website’s name, i snagged something that combined one of the names i went by and an alias i used online. it seemed good. folks had called me by it before. i have also been meeting new folks, and i find myself…self-conscious, of it, in a very specific sense: i don’t think it accurately evokes me. i consider it a bad name, because it has just…sat there, for a while. a name that describes a past state, not a present one; a relic.
