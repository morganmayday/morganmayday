There Is A Door

The anti-inductive knowledge necessary to change. A threshold, a barrier, a boundary, and none of the above. Related to the Arts of judgement and recognition. You make every choice you make.

My last attempt at describing the door.

I always find it difficult to define, describe, or even outline the door. It’s not something most folks are accustomed to thinking about. Ra’s piece on it is hauntingly good, and helped me when I was first linked it–I recommend it fairly regularly, but I find myself, again and again, wanting to point people to my own words, and finding them unwritten. Let’s fix that, shall we?

Required reading: Do As You Will and Emotions vs Reason, and Self Control–Caroline touches on a lot of concepts around this in those pieces.

Recommended reading: Ra’s piece of the same title, and Opening the Door (linked at the end of that piece).

When you lose control of yourself, who’s controlling you?

Ra, Opening the Door (Bold text for blue text, italic text for red text)

I’ve heard a couple theories in plural circles that the way society talks about “drunk me” or “angry me” or “hungry me” or “my inner (x)” or various other states as separate entities is indicative of more widespread plurality than anyone wants to acknowledge. It’s more indicative, really, of a habit of disavowing your own agency.

If your inner child, your inner pedant, your inner lesbian made that choice, you can’t really blame yourself for it. If you were drunk, angry, hungry, you can’t really blame yourself for it–well, you can blame yourself for losing control like that, but the choice…you were in an Altered State, not your Right Mind. Plural systems do this even harder, often; with the belief that consequences are rendered cruel because you’re affecting others than the one who made that choice.

We find ourselves making circular-or-inane-seeming statements about why this is wrong, often.

The truth is that every time you choose to hurt something, you choose to do that.

Caroline, Emotions vs Reason, and Self Control

If you are doing something, you want to do it.

Caroline, Do As You Will

These circle the door, but don’t quite…evoke it. “‘My X made me Y’ doesn’t mean you didn’t do Y, it just means you don’t want to have that agency” is helpful to convey that you can choose differently, but it doesn’t convey why or how.

Emotions vs Reason is a dichotomy almost anyone will understand intuitively.
