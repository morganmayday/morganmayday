a game that is a garden

I lost three longterm friends and nearly lost a fourth, over the back half of 2025, over who I was becoming. I had changed. I had sharpened myself. I had begun the process of becoming something new. When those losses happened, I told myself I would learn what I could and move on, that I wouldn’t let the anger of those who had once loved me damage what I thought of myself.

I was, of course, lying. Each of those losses was a knife to the chest. I took them harder than I admitted, either to myself or to others, and I took more than the valid criticisms to heart.

When Ansuz said I’d changed, when they said I’d become cruel and callous, when they said I hurt others for no reason, they were wrong, and they were right. When I made incisive observations about others, I did it to their faces, under a policy of honest communication and with very little regard for if it was the correct thing to do. I optimised for social cohesion, and I broke that social cohesion when I thought doing so would benefit it in the long run. Instead of making changes predicated on the real issues, I internalised what Ansuz had said. I softened my analyses for the consumption of their targets. I picked fewer fights over things I ostensibly valued. I optimised harder for social cohesion.

When Bean said I’d become a tyrant on my projects, she was wrong, and she was right. I had built a hierarchy, wherein my projects were mine and all other voices were secondary. I had justification to do so, of course, both that I could identify—those projects were mine, were the playground I couldn’t conceive of leaving behind and the art I had worked on for years and years while others had come and gone—and those I couldn’t, that I thought they were worth something, as a tool, as a method of soft propaganda, that my Artistic Vision alone could externalise into a usable form. Instead of biting the bullet and actually making those projects collaborative, I delegated, and kept a lead maintainer role in which I had final say. Instead of taking a step back and recognising what I wanted those projects to do as unsuitable, I clung tightly to the praxis I thought they were.

When Melanie said I treated my ostensible friends and loved ones as projects, she was more right than I thought she was. I had a hard policy of extracting myself from relationships I found myself as Project Managing, and yet I didn’t have a good handle on what those were. I internalised that I needed to be softer with my friends and loved ones, that the problem wasn’t the approach, but the flavour. I internalised that I shouldn’t have any projects, but I didn’t stop doing so; I stepped back from a critical lens and into an available one. I tried to turn myself from a leader into a fellow member of the friendgroup, but I tried to do so by softening myself into something consumable. This, to be clear, was not what I believe she was asking of me; it is, nevertheless, what I did in response to her criticism.

When Calcy said I was too clinical, that I was too detached, she was right, and she was wrong. I internalised that in the same way I did Melanie’s criticism; I stepped back from a critical lens and made myself consumable. I changed my speech patterns for social cohesion, but I also overcorrected from clinicality—I locked myself out of the big picture lens, robbed myself of precision and clarity for the sake of softness, compromised on my values for the sake of not being sharp against those I cared about. When she told me I hadn’t learned as much as I thought I had, when she told me my conversations had been had before, when she decried the identity magic I’d practised as “self-referencial [sic] esoteric pseudo-psychology” to pad the progress I’d actually made, she evoked insecurities I’d kept to myself as fact, and I internalised them as such. I began to hedge more, stopped discussing things others had discussed except when I saw it absolutely necessary, I stopped discussing identity magic almost at all and drastically scaled back my practice. I stopped trusting myself the way I had before, because someone I had once loved and respected had said that I didn’t deserve that trust.

The worst part of all of this is that I didn’t notice what I was doing to myself.

In October 2025, I identified a problem in a Discord server I was in. Several acquaintances of mine, who I liked interacting with, were being given heavy social incentive to neither grow nor change, to calcify in mental illness and trauma and stay As Is. Several of them were being actively predated upon. This was something I felt the need to address.

My first choice, addressing the predator situation at the root and changing the server culture of calcification, was nonviable. While I was an established presence in the community, I had very little social capital. I had lost most of it when I’d started standing up for myself and calling people on their bullshit, and I hadn’t had much in the first place as someone who was not sexually available, willing to be sexually available, or a man.

I reached, instead, for a separate solution. I established a server for myself, with an intentional “open secret” of my intents—I spoke at length about my expectations for myself, as something that I wanted to grow and change, and made it clear that I expected others to also grow and change. I lampshaded the fact that this was a petri dish, a project, a place in which I would expect willingness to grow, and a place where I would do my best to facilitate that growth. I told myself that I had succeeded in communicating this, even as I never said it outright.

This project succeeded, to a point. I do not regret that these individuals are my friends now; I do not regret the progress that they have made. I am proud of them, for what they have grown through, for leaving situations that were actively harming them, for choosing to change.

What I regret is that I made of them projects, and that I made of myself a mentor. What I regret more is how long I lied to myself about it.

I have spent a significant portion the last six years, give or take, writing a single tabletop roleplaying game lately called Kindling Age. It has been my primary project; a world, a game, and a story.

Within this project, there was a thing called the Garden. The Garden was, among other things, a metaphor for the game itself. It was meant to be a comment, a tongue-in-cheek look back at the camera, about the fact that Kindling Age was a game we were playing together. Those who inhabited it had problems, had varied ideals, had flaws, but were built to be…aspirational, in their ways: immortal anarchists who tried to course correct, to enable others, to build something new without taking the agency of those who had to live within it. At the same time, I cast them as ramical, as stewards, as rule-makers.

By giving them that role, I reflected what I had done to myself. I had sandboxed myself out of genuine radicalism. I had built myself projects within which I led others because it was how I accomplished my vision, and proceeded to be ineffective in accomplishing that vision. I had become a thing that I hated, and, on some level, I knew it.

I developed an apathy to the story I was telling; at the same time, I leaned in harder to the points in which I was saying something I thought needed to be said. I built games to teach others those things.

I made a game, and I made a garden, and I devoted myself to it, eventually to the exclusion of action outside of it. I sought to teach lessons in parable. I spent, as a conservative estimate, thousands if not tens of thousands of hours doing so in various forms, and even if I had been doing it exclusively for the sake of play, I did not enjoy a large portion of it.

I have come to believe that this was a waste.

I have sought to be a propagandist for some time now. I have looked to make radicals of those who do not think they can be. I have sought to help those I love and those I hate alike see what is wrong with the world, and see what can be done.

I’ve been thoroughly ineffective. You cannot make someone a radical, as morrigan has reminded me—they must do the work themselves. I have forsaken more productive projects and tasks for the sake of games, of trying to help others I see myself in be like me in this way. I have been captured by it, enthralled by the task of assisting others, of making art that makes others think about the values that underlie it. Overall, I have been most effective in this task simply by existing as myself and talking about it.

A few days ago, I was talking to some friends about where I was with myself and the projects I had. In the middle of doing so, I identified that I had softened myself, and morrigan asked if I’d read Hero Capture. I said not recently, and asked her to explain. She suggested I read it later instead, so of course I pulled out my phone and found the essay to read right then.

Have you ever been gripped by a work? Have you ever looked at something someone has said, with surely no way to know you would ever read it, and feel that you have been seen? Have you felt an author name what is happening, truly, and what you have done to yourself?

And so you have made your last hard pivot, and decay from agent into maintainer of a game that is a garden. Ziz, Hero Capture

I have, in my projects and the way I’ve responded to criticism, done this to myself. I have become not a knife to be wielded by myself, but a set of pruning shears to be wielded by others. I have let my values become something I do not fight for, instead hoping to instill them in others. I have tried to make not change, but instead those who desire it. This has been a mistake.

There is room in my life for play, but I can’t conflate it with praxis like I have, can’t prioritise it like it is an engine of change. There is room in my life to help others, but to treat that act as though it will manufacture radicals or save the world is to lie. To optimise for social cohesion above the things I tell myself I value is to value social cohesion above them. I have steeped myself in dishonesty. I have harnessed my energy into things that either further my values in minute enough amounts that I could convince myself they were worth doing, or did not further the values I wish to prioritise whatsoever.

I wrote this piece for two reasons.

First, I needed to communicate what’s going on to my friends and loved ones (hi). This isn’t the entire story, because I value my privacy, but I feel like I need to communicate at least this much to the majority of the folks I consider friends. I value clear communication. I don’t want to just…recede from things, wrap up Kindling Age as a project of mine, without explanation to those I care about.

Second, I needed to get my thoughts in a row. I’d thought all of this, sure, but I needed to assemble it into a form that made linear sense. I needed to construct for myself a reminder of exactly what went wrong and where. I needed to ensure that I learn from my mistakes. If I do not learn from my mistakes, I will continue being ineffective. I value my sharpness.

I am still coalescing exactly what my plans are. I don’t have total answers on that. For a very long time, these have been my purpose, these harnesses for my energy. I’m still figuring out where I’m headed from here, how I’m going to work to further my ideals now.

This, in its way, hurts, of course. I feel indignant at how I realised this, frustrated that it took me so long, worried, on some level, that I’ll fuck it up again, that I’ll lose friends over these changes, but…more than all that, I feel free again. I feel hopeful. More than that, I feel determined.

I’m looking forwards to this.
