Sometimes, you just feel like shit. This is, almost without exception, a solvable problem.

When you feel like shit, when you find yourself going "ughhhhhhhh" about everything, when everything feels big and disastrous and you feel like you need to *rot,* you have to ask yourself some questions.

## What questions should I be asking?

1) Have I taken all of my medication?\
Yes, all of it, not just the Immediately Applicable stuff. Your body and mind are inextricable, to malnourish one is to malnourish the other. This includes OTC stuff, not just your prescriptions---if you're hurting, take a painkiller, if you're vegan and don't eat seaweed, make sure you've taken your B12, et cetera.

2) a) Have I eaten in the last two to three hours?\
If you haven't eaten recently, that is a major thing to look to. Your body needs calories to function right. Your body and mind are, again, inextricable, to malnourish one is to malnourish the other. If you're not hungry, and it's been over six hours since you've eaten, find something to choke down. If you can, go for protein and carbs. It really does help. 

2) b) Have I had fluids in the last hour?\
If you haven't had water or water alternative such as Sports Drink, have water or water alternative such as Sports Drink. Being hydrated is a massive help to dealing with almost any issue.

3) Have I slept a) well b) enough?\
If you have not slept in the past twenty-four hours or more, it is, unequivocally, a good idea to take some rest. If you can't sleep, even just closing your eyes for 15 minutes helps---the Mythbusters did this one and it's lived rent free in my mind ever since. If you struggle sleeping well, I fucking hate that the common advice is right, but it is---try putting down screens at least 15 minutes before bed. A book, drawing for a little, anything you can do in one place with little physical effort and no screens. White noise can help a lot.

4) Have I made myself clean?\
If you haven't showered in the last two days, shower. If you haven't shaved in the last three days, shave. If you haven't changed clothes today, change clothes. Keeping yourself clean is shockingly important to your mental health.

5) Have I made my space clean?\
It doesn't necessarily have to be *tidy*, though that helps significantly. Getting trash thrown out, stuff taken off the floor, sheets and bedding washed, laundry put away, cups and plates taken to the kitchen and washed---all of this does, in fact, contribute. A clean space helps reduce psychological clutter.

6) Have I touched grass?\
I cannot understate the effect of leaving your immediate situation and going and sitting in the grass somewhere. If you can't go sit in the grass somewhere due to outside conditions, try a local library. If you don't have a local library or can't leave the house, open your blinds and stare outside for a while. 

7) Have I had quality time with myself, or my loved ones?\
If you haven't hung out with your loved ones in a few days, try to slice some time with them if you can. If you've been busy and around people a whole lot, try to slice some time to do a solitary activity.

## Why do these work?

I've referred to myself as a machine powered by a dynamo of will. A dynamo converts rotary kinetic energy to electrical current---the dynamo you're most likely to have used is a handcrank flashlight. I like dynamos. They're not as efficient or reliable as alternators, but I like em. There's a charm to it, to me.

Take a step back and think of yourself as a machine, for a moment. See where your systems interlock, see where they grind. Your body, your mind, your soul: all are part of _you._ Neglecting one neglects the others. These questions are designed to find the parts of yourself that need to be maintenanced. If you do not schedule systems maintenance, your systems will schedule it for you, and all of these systems interlock. As just one example, if your space isn't clean, the psychological clutter will make you less likely to remember to do other maintenance. Doing one when your systems are flagging, on the other hand, will start spinning up the dynamo. You can use that momentum.

When the world feels like it's spiraling, when it feels like I've slipped a bearing and am running *wrong,* I've got to start asking myself these questions and more. The alternative is rot.
